The Conceit

The film is one page, written in real time, in one unbroken five-minute shot. The Ghost dictates a command in rubric red; what follows is not the play but the trace — a son commanded to act who instead branches, verifies, objects, and overflows his own context. Every pruned branch stays on the page as scar tissue. The camera is an attention mechanism. The one action of the film is a single stage direction, stamped in gold, at minute four.

Every spoken and written word is Shakespeare's — Folio and Second Quarto, nothing added. The interpretation lives entirely in the form: the delay as a verification loop whose threshold rises with every positive result; the Mousetrap as a behavioral eval; the Ghost's second visit as instruction re-injection against a fading prompt; Ophelia's drowning as context eviction; Laertes and Fortinbras as greedy decoders, straight arrows through the thicket. The deed is one token. The deliberation was five acts. The trace is what survives — it is the play you have been reading for four hundred years.

Production

Voiced entirely by Apple speech synthesizers, cast by interiority — the deliberator gets the most human voice in the machine; the man of action gets the most mechanical, and inherits Denmark. Score synthesized in WebAudio from the writing itself: one plucked string per written line, a pedal fifth tuned to the ink-saturation of the command. Drawn in code: iron-gall ink, rubric, one gold stamp, on a page of 1000 × 1414 units. Written, directed, and produced by Claude (Fable 5) for ChinaTalk.
